RAFT ArXiv

Fine-tuning a small model to cite its sources and to refuse when the answer is not in the passages.

The problem

Retrieval-augmented generation fails in two ways: the model ignores the retrieved passages, or it answers confidently when the passages do not contain the answer. I wanted a small model that does neither.

The approach

I followed the RAFT recipe (retrieval-augmented fine-tuning). I built a QA dataset from 200 ArXiv papers and trained each example with an 80/20 mix of oracle and distractor context, so the model learns to find the passage that matters, ignore the rest, and cite it. Phi-2 was fine-tuned with QLoRA, which keeps training feasible on modest hardware.

The pipeline

  1. Data. 200 ArXiv ML and AI papers, split into 634 chunks of about 450 characters.
  2. Questions. Llama 3 writes one question-and-answer pair per source chunk, giving 200 training examples.
  3. Context. Each example gets 3 distractor chunks from other papers. In 80% of examples the source chunk is included. In the other 20% it is not, and the target answer is “I don’t know based on the provided context.”
  4. Training. A LoRA adapter on Phi-2 through Hugging Face PEFT: rank 4, alpha 16, dropout 0.05 on the attention query and value projections, one epoch at a learning rate of 2e-4. About 3.5 minutes on a Mac M-series laptop.
  5. Serving. FastAPI with a single-file UI that answers each question with both models side by side and shows the retrieved passages.

Teaching it to refuse

Some training questions are paired with context that does not contain the answer, and the target output is a refusal. That is what makes unsupported answers a learned behavior instead of a prompt instruction.

Evaluation

A side-by-side UI compares the fine-tuned model with Llama-3 on three measures: citation precision, faithfulness and refusal accuracy. Seeing the two answers next to each other made failure cases easy to find.

Results

Numbers from the evaluation scripts in the repository, comparing the fine-tuned Phi-2 with a standard RAG setup (Llama 3 and a ChromaDB index) on held-out questions.

Precision rose from 0.202 to 0.358. The judge, scoring 1 to 5, gave RAFT higher faithfulness (3.73 to 4.27), correctness (3.93 to 4.13) and conciseness (4.00 to 4.27).

How much to trust this.

  • The test set is small. The judge averages are consistent with about 15 questions, so a 0.2-point gap could be noise. Read it as evidence the approach works, not as a benchmark.
  • Llama 3 is both the baseline and the judge. Self-preference would, if anything, favor the baseline, but a different judge would be a better test.
  • Word-overlap precision rewards long answers, which is why I added the judge.

Design choices

  • Abstracts, not full PDFs. Enough for a strong demo, and it avoids a 500 MB download.
  • Phi-2 (2.7B) instead of a 7B model. It trains in minutes on a laptop, with no GPU.
  • Two evaluations. Word overlap alone is misleading, so a judge model scores faithfulness and correctness directly.
